# Benchmark Methodology Audit — 2026-08-17

This is a methodology audit, not a new performance campaign. It has two
layers. The first is a code-level review of `bench/runner.py`,
`bench/client.py`, `bench/report.py`, `bench/telemetry.py`,
`bench/annotations.py`, and `bench/llamacpp_perplexity.py`, done without
result data. The second layer tests each code-level hypothesis against the
sanitized evidence corpus published in `evidence/` on `origin/main` (commit
`c756d45`, "Publish sanitized benchmark evidence"), generated by the new
`bench/evidence.py` exporter from the git-ignored `results/` directory. That
corpus was not merged into this branch at audit time; it was read read-only
via `git archive origin/main -- evidence`, and no working-tree state was
changed. The corpus covers 205 runs (98 complete, 70 partial, 20 nonterminal,
13 aborted, 4 legacy-terminal), 6 matrices, and 4 new campaigns
(`moe-bandwidth-20260817T1539Z`, two experimental-NInfer campaigns, and one
NInfer admission failure) not yet described in any prose report.

Some hypotheses from the code-only pass held up against real numbers; one
did not. Both outcomes are recorded here, because the point of pairing code
review with data is to let evidence overturn a plausible-sounding guess.

## 1. Ordering/Thermal Confound — Corrected

Every matched baseline/variant pair in the 2026-08-17 campaign ran
baseline-then-variant back to back with no counterbalancing and no cooldown,
confirming the design flaw: Qwen3.6 smoke (baseline 06:10:55Z → MTP2
06:12:40Z), D256 (06:13:19→06:14:34), quick (06:15:34→06:17:07),
long-context (06:18:41→06:28:12), core (08:54:10→09:07:07), chat-quality
(09:17:37→09:18:17); Qwen3.8 262K baseline ends its journal at ~08:21:24 and
MTP5 starts 08:21:57, a 33-second gap; Muse-Glimmer smoke
(10:02:37→10:05:24) and quick (10:06:12→10:08:23).

Measuring actual per-case minimum and mean SM clock and temperature from the
raw per-second telemetry (`evidence/runs/*/telemetry-NNNN.json`, which
`bench/report.py`'s `_telemetry_summaries()` never computes — it only ever
stores `peak_sm_clock_mhz`/`peak_temperature_c`) shows the confound is far
too small to explain the published throughput and latency deltas:

| Pair | Baseline SM (min/mean/max) | Variant SM (min/mean/max) | Delta |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Qwen3.6 D256 (n=21/16) | 2502/2536/2554 | 2522/2533/2535 | mean −0.1% |
| Qwen3.6 quick (n=37/33) | 2476/2507/2535 | 2470/2507/2535 | none |
| Qwen3.6 long-context (n=503/531) | 2333/2427/2522 | 2327/2419/2502 | mean −0.36% |
| Qwen3.6 core (n=629/458) | 2418/2501/2515 | 2418/2493/2535 | mean −0.32%, +2.7 °C |
| Qwen3.8 262K (n=1729/1797) | 2288/2393.3/2502, 83.2 °C | 2281/2388.2/2509, 83.2 °C | mean SM −0.21%, temp identical |
| Muse-Glimmer quick (n=65/26) | 2470/2511/2535 | 2424/2487/2535 | min SM −1.9%, mean temp +3.8 °C |

The largest effect found anywhere (Qwen3.6 core: −0.32% mean SM clock, +2.7
°C) is two orders of magnitude too small to produce the published 29–56%
decode-throughput gains — those gains are unaffected by ordering. More
importantly, the one penalty this audit specifically suspected of being
thermal in origin — Qwen3.8 MTP5's TTFT/E2E penalty growing from 3.2% at 32K
tokens to 5.1% at 245.76K tokens — is directly refuted as thermal: mean
temperature is bit-for-bit identical between arms (83.2 °C both), and mean
SM clock differs by only 0.21%. That penalty has to come from a real
algorithmic source (speculative-decode verification or draft overhead
scaling with context/KV-cache size), not heat.

The one real thermal-carryover signature found is Muse-Glimmer DFlash15
quick (min SM clock −1.9%, mean temperature +3.8 °C) — but that pair's
throughput comparison was already excluded from any load-bearing conclusion
because all 32 quick responses failed content validation.

Mid-run throttling itself is real and directly visible — in the 245,760-token
needle case, SM clock climbs to ~2450 MHz by t=12s as temperature passes
80 °C, then sags to a sustained ~2346–2360 MHz floor (about a 4% drop) once
temperature holds at 84–86 °C. This happens symmetrically in both arms of
every matched pair, and minimum SM clock declines monotonically as context
size grows in both baseline and variant runs alike (Qwen3.6:
2457→2431→2398→2333 baseline vs. 2450→2418→2385→2327 MTP2; Qwen3.8:
2411→2379→2327→2288 baseline vs. 2405→2359→2307→2281 MTP5) — the same shape,
offset by only 5–8 MHz, in both arms. This is genuine dynamic clock scaling
under sustained load, not an ordering artifact, and it affects both arms of
every pair equally.

**Recommendation:** counterbalance future matched pairs (A/B/B/A) as good
practice, but do not revisit the 2026-08-17 conclusions on this basis — the
measured confound is too small to have produced them.

## 2. `decode_tps` Bundling Bias — Confirmed, Quantified, Non-Load-Bearing

`bench/client.py`'s `stream_chat_request` computes
`decode_tps = max(completion_tokens - 1, 0) / (finished - first_output_at)`,
assuming the first SSE emission carried exactly one token. The harness
separately records `emission_event_count` per sample, which lets the
assumption be tested directly.

Bundling turns out to be a property of speculative decoding, not of a
serving backend as such:

| Backend / mode | n samples | Median ratio (emissions/token) | Worst case |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| llama.cpp (incl. its own MTP/DFlash) | 1,155 | 1.0000 | 0.906 |
| vLLM, non-speculative | 1,047 | 1.0000 | 0.859 |
| vLLM, MTP/DFlash | 316 | 0.3477 | 0.106 |
| SGLang, non-speculative (Phi-4) | 102 | 1.0000 | 0.999 |
| SGLang, DSpark | 85 | 0.3398 | 0.297 |
| Ollama | 913 | 0.8558 | 0.886 (excl. one degenerate sample) |

llama.cpp emits one SSE event per accepted token even under its own
speculative paths. vLLM and SGLang stay near 1.0 under plain autoregressive
decode but collapse to a ~0.33–0.35 median (worst case one emission per 9.4
tokens) the instant speculative decoding activates.

Checking the six published decode/throughput headlines individually:

- **Qwen3.6 MTP2 D256 (+53.7%)** — llama.cpp, ratio 0.997–1.0. Negligible.
- **Qwen3.8 DSpark SGLang C8 (127.18 tok/s)** — worst single sample
  (`20260817T065612Z-qwen38-27b-nvfp4-dspark-sglang-core-6b79826a`,
  concurrency-8, sample 75: 256 completion tokens, 77 emission events) could
  overstate its own per-sample `decode_tps` by roughly 235% (3.36×). But the
  127.18 tok/s **headline is `completion_tokens / case_wall_s`**
  (`bench/report.py:334`), which never reads `emission_event_count` and is
  mathematically untouched by the bug.
- **Muse-Glimmer DFlash15 D128 (+406%)** — llama.cpp, ratio 0.953 both arms.
  Max theoretical inflation ~5%, immaterial to a 406% claim.
- **Qwen3.8 Q5 quick/core** — llama.cpp, ratio 0.997–1.0. Negligible.
- **Qwen3.8 MTP5 262K long-context** — zero decode/concurrency-kind samples;
  it is a needle-retrieval TTFT/E2E comparison, not a decode headline. N/A.

The worst instance found anywhere in the corpus is not a headline case:
`20260816T100225Z-qwen3-coder-30b-a3b-bf16-dflash15-core-fdc2fd73`,
decode-1024, sample 9 (1,024 completion tokens, 109 emission events) — up to
847% overstatement of that one sample's `decode_tps`. The 2026-08-16 report
already flags this run's field as an estimate rather than a ground-truth
rate.

Every one of the six ranked headline claims is built on aggregate output
throughput (`completion_tokens / wall_s`), not the biased per-sample
`decode_tps`. The bias is real and large but does not flip any published
ranking. The risk is entirely downstream: `evidence/runs/*/samples.json` now
ships the biased `decode_tps` field as raw "evidence" without the caveat
text that accompanies it in prose.

**Recommendation:** fix the `-1` numerator assumption in
`stream_chat_request`, or attach a per-sample bundling flag
(`decode_estimate_one_token_chunks` already exists at the case-summary level
in `bench/report.py:322` — push an equivalent per-sample marker into
`samples.json`).

## 3. Annotation Coverage — Confirmed Gap, Ledger Undercounts

`bench/annotations.py` requires a human to run a CLI command to flag
interference; there is no automatic mid-run detection of a foreign GPU
process or container. The new exporter makes this worse in one specific way:
`bench/evidence.py` collapses every annotation to a bare count
(`measurement_annotations_count`, `startup_measurement_annotations_count`,
per-case `measurement_annotation_count`) and explicitly discards the
underlying reason, evidence, and timestamp text — `_safe_summary_tree`
(`bench/evidence.py:1573`) has a hardcoded skip for any `reason`/`evidence`/
`timestamp`/`case_id` key when the surrounding name contains `"annotation"`.
Confirmed empirically:
`evidence/runs/20260816T052746Z-rlm-qwen3-8b-bf16-reasoning-quick-c7491fa4/`
shows `startup_measurement_annotations_count: 1` with no further detail
recoverable from the sanitized bundle.

Scanning all 205 `summary.json` files for nonzero annotation counts finds
the four runs already named in the 2026-08-16 report's "Validity and
Evidence Ledger" — plus a fifth, `qwen36-35b-a3b-nvfp4-mtp3-core-795163dc`
(startup count = 1), which that report's own prose (§529, "a read-only audit
ended before `server_ready`") describes but the ledger's "four
interferences" tally never counts.

**Recommendation:** export annotation `reason` as a fixed, sanitized
categorical field (not free text) rather than collapsing to a count, and
correct the 2026-08-16 ledger to five interferences.

## 4. Perplexity Screen — Paired Data Exists but Isn't Exported as Pairs

The Q4/Q5/Q8/IQ2 WikiText-2 comparison (`--chunks 64 --ctx-size 512`) was
analyzed as an unpaired comparison with a ±0.124 uncertainty band that
swamps the 0.004–0.011 Q4/Q5/Q8 deltas, even though all four runs process
the identical 64 chunks in identical order — a paired per-chunk analysis
would have materially tighter power. `evidence/runs/*perplexity*/samples.json`
retains only cumulative per-chunk perplexity (`chunk_index`,
`cumulative_perplexity`), not per-chunk NLL deltas. A paired analysis is
still derivable — back out per-chunk log-likelihood from consecutive
cumulative values, since context size is fixed at 512 — but it requires
that reconstruction; it is not directly exported as ready-to-use paired
samples.

**Recommendation:** export per-chunk NLL deltas alongside the cumulative
value so a paired comparison doesn't require reconstruction, and raise
`--chunks` for any future quantization comparison intended to resolve
sub-0.01-PPL deltas.

## 5. Evidence Exporter Integrity

`bench/evidence.py` is a strict allowlist/passthrough validator — it
recomputes nothing (rates, aggregates, and counters all pass through
verbatim from the original journal), so no new aggregation-math bug was
introduced. Specific checks:

- **Telemetry fidelity:** `_project_telemetry` and `_telemetry_files` are
  1:1 passthroughs of every raw per-second row; the 2,000-sample chunk split
  (`TELEMETRY_SAMPLES_PER_CHUNK`) is a file-size boundary, not aggregation.
  `sm_clock_mhz` and `temperature_c` survive per row, which is what made
  Section 1's min/mean recomputation possible at all. The exporter's own
  *summary* layer (`_TELEMETRY_SUMMARY_FIELDS`) still carries only
  peak values, reproducing `bench/report.py`'s blind spot one layer up —
  but the raw layer is intact.
- **Status/event-count consistency:** sampled one run from each of
  complete/partial/nonterminal/aborted and found each manifest's
  `lifecycle.event_counts` and `status` mutually consistent;
  `_verify_run_bundle` (`bench/evidence.py:3115`) cross-checks
  `measurement_terminal == (terminal_event == "run_complete")` at export
  time.
- **Real information-loss gap:** `request_body_json` and any
  reasoning-effort/thinking-toggle field are entirely absent from the
  exporter (zero references in the file) even though they are real,
  per-model configuration (`bench/manifest.py`, `bench/client.py`) that
  determines whether a "thinking"-tagged model actually ran with reasoning
  enabled. A `manifest.json` for a `-reasoning-` run shows
  `model.tasks: ["chat","json","thinking"]` with no way to confirm from
  `evidence/` alone whether the request carried a `reasoning_effort` cap.

**Recommendation:** export `request_body_json` (or a derived, sanitized
reasoning-effort scalar) alongside the model's declared tasks.

## 6. Prose-vs-Evidence Reconciliation

Seventeen claim groups spanning both 2026-08-16 and 2026-08-17 reports were
checked against the sanitized bundle, prioritizing the highest-stakes
numbers: the Qwen3.6 MTP2 matched-core row (D256 +53.727% claimed vs.
+53.726% recomputed; C1/C2/C4/C8 all matched to six decimals, including
every speculative-decoding acceptance counter), the DSpark SGLang C8
127.18483005260319 tok/s figure, the full Q4/Q5/Q8/IQ2 perplexity table
(values, uncertainty terms, wall times, and telemetry all exact), the
Muse-Glimmer DFlash15 +406.174% D128 claim, and all four Qwen3.8 MTP5
262K-context TTFT/E2E tiers (e.g. 245,760 tokens: 588.263535s → 588.263534587s
baseline, an exact match to the ninth significant figure).

16 of 17 groups matched exactly. The one apparent discrepancy (the Coder
AR-vs-DFlash15 *quick* prefill-approximation figures) reconciles once
prompt-token counts are normalized per request rather than summed across
the suite's three requests — the doc's documented method, not drift. Zero
qualitative mismatches were found (pass/fail counts, validation flags, and
status fields all agreed). No transcription or fabrication issue exists in
either report.

**Recommendation:** treat any future doc/evidence delta greater than 0.5%
as a real regression signal rather than measurement noise, given this
demonstrated baseline of exact fidelity.

## 7. MoE `llama-bench` Sweep (`moe-bandwidth-20260817T1539Z`)

This campaign uses `llama-bench` directly — a raw prompt/generation
throughput tool, not the HTTP-server harness — across five GGUF/Ollama
artifacts plus a dense control, and backs the "same-binary kernel panel"
paragraph in `docs/moe-landscape-2026-08-17.md`.

All headline TG1024 figures reproduce exactly from `measurements.json`'s
`avg_ts` field: dense Qwen3.8 27B control 10.964899 tok/s; the four sparse
artifacts (qwen3-30b-a3b 82.558599, qwen3.6-35b-a3b 61.563324,
nemotron-cascade-2 71.727115, nemotron35-lightning 66.94467) all fall in the
claimed 61–83 tok/s band, reproducing ratios up to 7.529×. Fairness holds at
the settings level: `n_batch`/`n_ubatch` (8192/512), `flash_attn` (1),
`n_threads` (20), KV cache types (q8_0/q8_0), `n_gpu_layers` (-1),
`split_mode` (layer) are single-valued across all 40 rows — no artifact ran
under different serving conditions. `n_cpu_moe` is 0 for every row; no
artifact silently offloaded experts to CPU. Muse-Glimmer, included in the
same manifest as a dense control rather than a sixth MoE result, sits at
11.763 tok/s next to the dense control rather than the sparse band,
consistent with the doc's own framing.

The gap is thermal verification: within the sweep's single capture file,
the dense control ran immediately before the sparse artifacts (dense-then-
sparse, back to back — the same un-counterbalanced pattern as Section 1),
and the doc's mitigation is two bracketing throughput samples
(`control-pre`/`control-post`: 11.004→10.968 tok/s, −0.328%;
`muse-control-pre`/`muse-control-post`: 10.599→10.458, −1.33%), both
described as "inside the 5% gate." But **this campaign's own
`telemetry.json` records `capture_count: 0`** — no per-second GPU telemetry
exists for it at all, so the drift claim has no clock/temperature signal
behind it, only the two throughput brackets. Separately, every row runs
exactly 5 repetitions; the noisiest (PP128, qwen3-30b-a3b) has an 8.04%
relative standard deviation, while TG1024 rows are far tighter (0.05–0.45%).
The doc's uniform six-decimal precision therefore overstates what n=5
actually supports, though the sparse/dense gaps (5.6–7.5×) are 15–100×
larger than the noise band regardless.

**Recommendation:** wire up telemetry capture for the `llama_bench`
campaign type (currently silently absent), and round published throughput
to 2–3 significant figures to match n=5 precision.

## 8. Experimental NInfer Port

Three campaigns cover an experimental port of
[`Neroued/ninfer`](https://github.com/Neroued/ninfer) to DGX Spark's
`sm_121a` (see `patches/ninfer/README.md`), narrated in
`docs/moe-landscape-2026-08-17.md`.

The headline percentage gains reconcile exactly to raw measurements:
`ninfer-experimental-sm121a-20260817T181134Z`'s `tg1024` captures give
`decode_output_tok_s_mean` 67.69186988 (MTP0) → 140.1902588 (MTP3), a
107.10058541% delta matching the doc's 107.100585% to six decimal places;
`ninfer-qwen38-nvfp4-sm121a-20260817T200147Z` gives 11.68134009 → 21.22158673,
an 81.67082344% delta matching 81.670823%. Both campaigns record 5 measured
repetitions with 1 discarded warmup, applied identically to both arms
(verified directly from the five per-repetition decode-second values), and
the non-MTP-related configuration (`prefill_chunk: 128`, `use_cuda_graph:
false`, `kv_cache: int8-group64`, context size) is identical between MTP0
and MTP3 arms in both campaigns — the doc's "matched eager" claim holds.

Two things need sharper framing than the doc currently gives them. First,
the "parity" check in each campaign's manifest (`token_ids_equal`,
`output_bytes_equal`, 6–8 output tokens) compares the port's own MTP0 greedy
output against its own MTP3 greedy output — there is no external reference
implementation anywhere in this check (no comparison to upstream NInfer, to
llama.cpp, or to vLLM, and no logit- or perplexity-level check). It is a
regression/determinism smoke test internal to the port, not evidence that
the port's numerics match any outside oracle, and 6–8 tokens is too short a
window to catch drift that could appear over a full 1,024-token generation.
Second, the executive-summary claim that "the Qwen3.6 CUDA Graph attempt
failed before measurement" (citing 1,107,603,456 bytes consumed against
12,582,912 planned) has **no corresponding record in the exported
evidence** — `ninfer-experimental-sm121a-20260817T181134Z/measurements.json`
contains only five records (artifact-inspect, admission-mtp0,
native-eager-mtp0, native-eager-mtp3, image-inspect), none of them this
failure. It rests entirely on narrative prose. Separately,
`ninfer-gb10-20260817` (`failure_codes:
["unsupported_compute_architecture","cuda_toolkit_too_old"]`) is a distinct,
earlier pair of stock-configure-time rejections (arch gate, then CUDA
version gate) and should not be conflated with the later CUDA Graph failure
the doc also describes — they are two different failed attempts.

**Recommendation:** add a minimal capture record (bytes-consumed vs.
bytes-planned, timestamp) for the CUDA Graph failure so it carries the same
scalar backing as every other NInfer claim, and state explicitly in prose
that the parity check has no external oracle.

## 9. Corpus-Wide Failure Taxonomy

Categorizing all 33 aborted/nonterminal runs by `lifecycle.failure` surfaces
two clusters not discussed in either benchmark-results report:

- **A 6-attempt TensorRT-LLM audio crash loop.** Five aborted attempts at
  `phi-4-multimodal-instruct-fp8-trtllm-audio` (`DirectTrtllmWorkerError`)
  plus a sixth that hung mid `worker_start` and never reached a terminal
  event, before a seventh attempt finally succeeded. The 2026-08-16 report
  documents only that seventh, successful run; the six preceding failures
  exist only as bare run-id references, not narrative.
- **A 14-run Ollama non-completion cluster.** Fourteen Ollama smoke runs
  across 9 distinct models, clustered in a roughly 9-minute window on
  2026-08-14 (21:10–21:18Z), several duplicated at two timestamps 7–12
  minutes apart, none reaching `run_complete` and none carrying a `failure`
  object (a distinct signature from the documented Ollama serialized-queue
  and `eval_duration` issues). This looks like a bulk harness or process
  interruption specific to the early sweep and is not discussed anywhere.

Three additional `RuntimeErrorWithContext` aborts at `server_start`
(`gemma-3-4b-it-bf16-quick`, `nemotron-labs-diffusion-8b-bf16-quick`,
`nemotron-labs-diffusion-14b-bf16-quick`) were never retried anywhere in the
205-run corpus and represent permanent coverage gaps for those three quick
matrices, distinct from four other `server_start` aborts that were silently
retried minutes later and succeeded (one of which — `qwen3-vl-embedding-2b`
— is reported in prose as if its first attempt worked cleanly).

Of the 70 partial runs, 40 are driven by benign
`case_skipped_unsupported`/context-limit skips. Of the remaining 30 with a
real `BenchmarkRequestError`, roughly 23 match the documented Ollama
`eval_duration` prefill bug, and 5 match the documented xgrammar
`normalize_tool_choice` bug — but 3 run on vLLM/SGLang, not Ollama
(`gpt-oss-120b-mxfp4-quick-01441f96`,
`phi-4-multimodal-instruct-fp8-quick-65e98f95`,
`phi-4-multimodal-instruct-fp8-reasoning-core-7a48a87e`), where the
Ollama-specific explanation cannot apply. These are reasoning-capable models
failing 1-token prefill probes — the same "reasoning consumes the whole
budget, visible content stays empty" mechanism already documented for
DSpark and Muse-Glimmer. The reasoning-budget/empty-content failure
therefore generalizes beyond the two cases already flagged in the
2026-08-17 report, though it remains a small tail (~3 of 70 partial runs),
not the dominant corpus-wide cause.

**Recommendation:** add narrative coverage for the TensorRT-LLM audio crash
loop and the Ollama non-completion cluster, and note the three permanently
uncovered quick matrices as open gaps rather than silent absences.

## Consolidated Recommendations

1. Export `request_body_json` (or a derived reasoning-effort scalar)
   alongside model tasks in `bench/evidence.py`.
2. Export annotation `reason` as a categorical field instead of collapsing
   to a count; correct the 2026-08-16 ledger to five interferences.
3. Add min/mean SM clock to `bench/report.py`'s `_telemetry_summaries()` —
   the raw per-second data already supports it.
4. Fix, or add a per-sample bundling flag for, `stream_chat_request`'s
   `decode_tps` numerator assumption.
5. Export per-chunk NLL deltas in the perplexity screen to enable a paired
   comparison without reconstruction.
6. Wire up telemetry capture for the `llama_bench` campaign type.
7. Counterbalance future matched A/B pairs (A/B/B/A) as good practice going
   forward — not because it invalidated anything measured so far.
8. Add narrative coverage for the TensorRT-LLM audio crash loop and the
   14-run Ollama non-completion cluster; state plainly that the NInfer
   parity check has no external oracle.
